Options backtest · AI-linked equities

Does buying volatility pay when AI stocks crash?

Seventeen option strategies on ten volatile AI names plus SMH and QQQ, rolled daily-marked from to . Each one was rerun from every possible roll-start day and under three implied-vol assumptions. The page ends with a stress test on today's actual option prices.

Bottom line

Rolling puts, all the time−2% to −4.5% / yr

Spending 10% of capital per year on AI-basket puts lost 20–45% of the premium each year on average. Puts are insurance and carry an insurance premium.

…but in a sudden crash+50%

The same 10%/yr budget in 20%-OTM, 3-month puts made about +50% on the whole portfolio during the COVID crash, while the stocks fell 35%.

Slow bear markets≈ 0%

In the grinding 2022 sell-off the stocks fell 38%, and the same tail puts made under +1%. Each roll repriced at higher vol and the drops came too gradually.

Today's entry price~2× normal

Implied vol is very elevated right now (SMH ~64%, QQQ ~33%). A hedge bought today needs a bigger, faster drop to pay than at any calm point in the sample.

Volatility by itself doesn't make option buyers money. On average, implied vol is priced above the vol that actually shows up, so a buyer profits only from a move bigger and faster than the market expects. For a cyber-shock thesis, the structures that fit are short-dated-to-3-month tail puts or put spreads on SMH/QQQ, sized as a budget you can lose entirely. The backtest's one cheap way to cut the cost was buying only when implied vol is low. That filter would not allow an entry today.

Expiry

How it was built

  • Prices: daily split-adjusted closes, VIX and 13-week T-bill from Yahoo Finance, 2018 onward.
  • Option prices are simulated with Black-Scholes, because free historical option chains don't exist. ATM implied vol = VIX × (stock's 120-day realised vol ÷ SPY's), scaled so implied variance averages 1.15× realised (1.35× for ETFs). That is the usual variance risk premium. Smile shape is fitted to today's chains.
  • Costs: half bid/ask of 2–5% of the option price per leg, depending on liquidity, plus commission. Expiring options settle at intrinsic value.
  • Robustness: each strategy was run from every roll-start offset (21 for monthly, 21 for quarterly in 3-day steps) and at three vol levels. That is backtests in total.
  • Code and CSVs: aibt/ results/ in the repo.

What this can't tell you

  • No backtest can price a hypothetical cyber attack. Past crashes are stand-ins, and only the COVID crash was fast enough to resemble a sudden shock.
  • Simulated vol misses real-world details: earnings jumps, the skew steepening in a sell-off, and wider spreads in a panic.
  • The stock list is hindsight-picked AI winners. That inflates buy-and-hold, covered calls and short puts.
  • Anthropic isn't publicly traded and has no listed options. This tests a general AI-sector drawdown, not any one company.
  • This is research, not financial advice. Options can expire worthless, and short options can lose much more than the premium collected.
